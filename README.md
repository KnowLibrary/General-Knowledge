## Why Silver Price is increasing
The anticipated impact of a U.S. Federal Reserve (Fed) interest rate cut is generally favourable for gold and silver prices in terms of USD value.

1. Reduced Opportunity Cost
Gold and silver do not generate income such as interest or dividends, making them non-yielding assets. 
When the Fed lowers interest rates, returns on interest-bearing assets such as U.S. Treasuries, bank deposits, and other fixed-income instruments decline. Because these alternatives become less rewarding, investors are more inclined to hold precious metals, thereby increasing their appeal. 

2. Depreciation of the U.S. Dollar
Since gold and silver are globally traded in U.S. Dollars, their value is closely tied to USD strength. 
A rate cut commonly results in a weaker U.S. dollar because lower returns make dollar-denominated assets less attractive for global investors. With a softer dollar, precious metals become cheaper for buyers using other currencies, boosting demand and typically lifting their prices in USD terms. 

3. Inflation Outlook and Market Uncertainty
Monetary easing through rate cuts can increase inflationary pressures. Gold, known as a safeguard against inflation and currency erosion, tends to see higher demand during such periods. 
Rate cuts also signal potential economic stress or slowdown. This heightened uncertainty encourages investors to seek safe-haven assets such as gold. 

Implications for Silver (Industrial and Bullion Asset)
Silver responds to the same monetary dynamics as gold. However, because of its significant industrial usage in sectors like electronics and solar energy, a deep economic downturn (which may trigger the rate cut) could reduce silver’s industrial demand. This may limit its price rise or briefly push prices lower. Nonetheless, weaker interest rates and a softer dollar usually remain the dominant drivers of silver’s price movement, particularly when markets anticipate upcoming rate cuts.
